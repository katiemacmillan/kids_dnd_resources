Chapter 1: How to Play

Sanebs, you are a hero in Bounties Abound. The Dungeon Master (DM) describes the world. You say what Sanebs does. Then the dice help decide what happens. You are on a team — share the adventure with your friends.

The Golden Rule

If a special rule on this book or your character sheet disagrees with a basic rule, use the special rule. Sanebs's skeleton body and the Undead pact are special rules.

The d20 Check

When you try something risky, roll a 20-sided die (d20) and add the number the DM asks for.

  1. Roll a d20.
  2. Add your bonus (like +4 for Intimidation).
  3. Tell the DM the total.

If your total is equal to or higher than the Difficulty Class (DC), you succeed. The DM sets the DC. Easy jobs might be 10. Hard jobs might be 15 or 20.

Advantage & Disadvantage

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher number.

Disadvantage: roll two d20s and keep the lower number.

If you ever have both at once, they cancel. Just roll one d20.

Natural 20 and Natural 1

A 20 on the die is a critical success. Something extra cool happens. A 1 is a critical fail. Something goes wrong, but it can still be funny. Don't get mad at a 1. Lean into it.

Inspiration

If you play Sanebs in a fun, clever, or brave way, the DM may give you Inspiration. You can spend it later to roll with Advantage. You can only hold one Inspiration at a time.

Chapter 2: Your Turn

When a fight starts, everyone rolls Initiative: d20 + your Dexterity modifier. Sanebs, that is d20 + 3. Highest total goes first.

On Your Turn You Can:

  • Move up to 25 feet (your Speed).
  • Take 1 Action (Attack, Cast a Spell, Dash, Help, Hide, Search, and more).
  • Take 1 Bonus Action if a power says you can. Your Nimble Escape uses a Bonus Action.
  • Talk a little. A joke, a threat, or a warning is free.

You also get 1 Reaction between your turns. You might use it to make an Opportunity Attack if a foe runs away from you.

Useful Actions

  • Attack: hit with a weapon or a spell attack like Eldritch Blast.
  • Cast a Spell: use a cantrip or a spell slot.
  • Dash: move extra Speed this turn (another 25 feet).
  • Disengage: move away without Opportunity Attacks. You can do this as a Bonus Action with Nimble Escape.
  • Help: give a friend Advantage on their next check or attack.
  • Hide: Stealth check. You can do this as a Bonus Action with Nimble Escape.
  • Ready: wait for a trigger, then use your Reaction.

Hitting and Missing

Roll d20 + your attack bonus. If you meet or beat the target's Armor Class (AC), you hit. Then roll damage.

A natural 20 on an attack is a critical hit. Roll the damage dice twice, then add your bonus once.

Hit Points and Rest

You have 12 Hit Points. When you reach 0, you fall unconscious and start making Death Saving Throws (roll a d20: 10+ is a success). Three successes and you stabilize. Three failures and Sanebs dies. A 20 wakes you with 1 HP. A 1 counts as two failures.

A Short Rest is about 1 hour. You can spend Hit Dice to heal (you have 1d8). A Long Rest is about 8 hours. You get all HP and spell slots back.

Healing Magic Is Weird for You

Most healing spells (like Cure Wounds) don't work on undead. Spare the Dying doesn't help you either. Food, rest, and some special magic still can. Tell the healer: "I'm bones. Regular healing usually fizzles."

Chapter 3: Scores, Skills & Dice

Your six ability scores are who Sanebs is in numbers. The modifier is the number you add to rolls.

STR 15 (+2) DEX 16 (+3) CON 17 (+3) INT 14 (+2) WIS 14 (+2) CHA 17 (+3)

Your Proficiency Bonus is +2. Add it when you use a skill or weapon you are trained in. Your Armor Class is 13 — that is how hard you are to hit. You don't wear heavy armor.

Your Best Skills (+4)

  • Arcana: magic lore, weird symbols, "what is that glowing thing?"
  • Deception: bluffing. You are great at deadpan jokes that hide the truth.
  • Insight: spot a lie. You sat on a bench and listened to excuses all day.
  • Intimidation: the quiet stare of someone who used to decide fates.
  • Investigation: what really happened. You are still on the case.

Other checks still work. You just add the matching modifier (no extra +2).

Chapter 4: You Are a Halfling Skeleton

Halflings are small, lucky, and hard to pin down. You are also a skeleton. You look a lot like a grinning, hoodie-wearing pile of bones who should not be this chill.

You are Small. Your walking Speed is 25 feet. You see in dim light and darkness out to 60 feet (Darkvision). You speak Common and Halfling.

Lucky

When you roll a 1 on a d20 for an attack, check, or save, you can reroll the die. You must use the new roll. Fate likes you, even if you are undead.

Brave

You have Advantage on saving throws against being Frightened. It takes a lot to scare a halfling — even a skeletal one.

Halfling Nimbleness

You can move through the space of any creature that is a size larger than you. Slip between a giant's boots like it's nothing.

Nimble Escape

As a Bonus Action, you can Disengage or Hide. Duck behind a crate, slip past a guard, or vanish after a joke. You are slippery.

Relentless Endurance

Once per Long Rest, if you drop to 0 Hit Points but are not killed outright, you drop to 1 HP instead. Mark it on your sheet when you use it. You are stubborn bones.

Skeleton Body

  • Vulnerable to Bludgeoning. Clubs, hammers, and falling rocks hurt extra. The attacker rolls bludgeoning damage twice.
  • Immune to Poison damage and the Poisoned condition. Poison? You don't even have a stomach.
  • Immune to Exhaustion. You don't get worn out the way living people do.
  • Healing spells usually fail. You are undead, not a living patient.

Playing Your Look

You can shrug at danger, tell terrible puns, and act like nothing bothers you. Underneath, you still care about your friends. That mix is funny and cool.

Background: Judge

Before the hoodie and the blasting, Sanebs had a job. You were a judge.

You did not slam a giant gavel all day and yell "ORDER!" You were the quiet kind. People came in angry. You leaned back, hands in your sleeves, and listened. Then you said one short sentence, and the argument was over. That was the job: hear both sides, keep score, and tell the truth even when it made somebody mad.

Then you finished your last case... and you did not wake up. An Undead patron offered you a second job. Same work, weirder office. Now you keep score with magic. The sash is faded and hidden under your jacket. The tiny wooden gavel still lives in a pocket, just in case.

Feature: The Last Word

Old habits stick, even on bones. In a town, castle, or camp, if people are arguing about a crime, a rule, or who is lying, they often let you speak last — and they listen. You can also get a short meeting with a mayor, a captain of the guard, or a local judge by explaining you used to sit the bench.

This does not mean everyone obeys you. It means they give you a hearing. What they do after that is still up to them (and the dice).

How to Play a Judge

  • Talk in short sentences. Verdicts, not speeches.
  • Give people a chance. Then don't give them a second one if they waste it.
  • You hate bullies more than thieves. A thief might be hungry. A bully just likes hurting people.
  • When someone asks what you used to do, you can shrug: "sat. listened. decided."

Chapter 5: You Are a Warlock of the Undead

A warlock makes a deal with a powerful being. That deal is your pact. You don't study dusty books all day. You ask, you bargain, and weird magic answers.

Your magic uses Charisma. Spell save DC is 13. Spell attack bonus is +5.

Your Patron: The Undead

Your patron is the Undead — not "a random zombie," but a deathless ruler. Think a lich in a dusty crown, a vampire who never blinks, a mummy-king, or a ghost who still sits on a throne. They finished dying a long time ago and decided that was boring. Now a little of that "I don't stay down" power lives in you.

That fits Sanebs pretty well. You are already bones. Your patron is the reason those bones still walk, grin, and blast people.

The Deal

The Undead patron gives you magic that laughs at graves. In return, you are still a judge — just a harder one to bury. You do not have to be scary every second. You can nap. You can tell bone puns. When you do get serious, though, people should feel a chill.

Your patron didn't pick a random pile of bones. They picked someone who already knew how to keep score. They might whisper like a tired king, a smug vampire, or a pile of dust with opinions. Ask the DM what your Undead patron sounds like.

How Your Magic Looks

  • Eldritch Blast can look like a flying bone, a cold white-blue beam, or a lazy wink that knocks someone over.
  • Fire Bolt is a little funeral-candle spark from a "harmless" finger-gun.
  • Detect Evil and Good is your dead-radar. You can tell if something nearby is undead, fey, fiend, or holy.
  • When you take a shortcut (Nimble Escape), you blur like a ghost stepping through a gap that was not there.

Patron Gift: Form of Dread

Bonus Action. You can do this 2 times per Long Rest (your Proficiency Bonus). For 1 minute you put on your scariest skeleton face:

  • Your grin stretches. Your eyes go dark except for two pinpricks of light. You might float an inch off the ground. You still look like you — just the version nobody wants to fight.
  • You gain 1d10 + 1 temporary Hit Points (ghost HP that gets used up first).
  • You cannot be Frightened while the form lasts.
  • Once during the form, when you hit a creature with an attack, they make a Wisdom save (DC 13). If they fail, they are Frightened of you until the form ends. (Frightened creatures have Disadvantage on checks and attacks while they can see you, and they cannot willingly move closer to you.)

Form of Dread uses your Bonus Action. Nimble Escape also uses your Bonus Action. Pick one on a turn, not both.

Spells the Undead Likes

Warlocks of the Undead often get extra spell choices as they level up. You do not have these yet unless the DM says so:

  • 1st level: False Life (borrowed ghost HP) and Bane (an unlucky curse)
  • 2nd level: Blindness/Deafness and Phantasmal Force (a spooky trick the target believes)
  • 3rd level: Speak with Dead (yes, you can chat with bones) and Phantom Steed (a ghost horse shortcut)

What You Are Great At

  • Blasting from a safe distance with Eldritch Blast and Fire Bolt.
  • Talking your way through trouble (Deception and Intimidation).
  • Spotting cursed or holy things with Detect Evil and Good.
  • Staying on your feet when others would drop.

Pact Magic (Level 1)

You have 1 first-level spell slot. When you use it, it is gone until you finish a Short Rest or Long Rest. That is better than most casters, who need a long rest. You get fewer slots, but they come back faster.

Cantrips (Eldritch Blast and Fire Bolt) do not use slots. You can cast them every turn.

Growing Stronger

You are level 1. As you level up (usually through level 5 in this campaign), warlocks typically:

  • Level 2: Eldritch Invocations (small extra pact tricks) and a second spell slot.
  • Level 3: a Pact Boon (a special gift from your Undead patron — a spooky pet, a bone-blade, or a ghostly book).
  • Level 4: a little stronger and tougher.
  • Level 5: 3rd-level pact spells and a stronger Eldritch Blast (two beams).

The DM will tell you exactly what you gain. Don't worry about memorizing the future. Play the cool skeleton you are now.

Chapter 6: Your Magic

You know 2 cantrips and 1 first-level spell. Charisma is your spellcasting ability.

Cantrips (at will)

Eldritch Blast. Action. Range 120 feet. Spell attack +5. Hit: 1d10 force damage. This is your signature lazy beam. Force damage smashes almost anything. It can look like a flying bone.

Fire Bolt. Action. Range 120 feet. Spell attack +5. Hit: 1d10 fire damage. A flammable object you hit (that nobody is wearing) can catch fire.

1st-Level Spell (1 slot)

Detect Evil and Good. Action. Concentration, up to 10 minutes. For the duration you know if an aberration, celestial, elemental, fey, fiend, or undead is within 30 feet of you, and where it is. You also sense consecrated or desecrated places. Walls of earth and stone can block it. Great for "is that statue haunted?"

Weapons

Short Sword. Attack +4. Hit: 1d6 + 2 slashing. Use this if a monster is in your face and blasting is awkward.

A Simple Combat Plan

  1. Stay back. You only have 12 HP, and hammers hurt extra.
  2. Blast with Eldritch Blast or Fire Bolt. Pop Form of Dread if the fight looks scary.
  3. If you need to slip away, Bonus Action Nimble Escape (Disengage or Hide).
  4. Save Detect Evil and Good for creepy rooms, not every hallway.

Chapter 7: Quick Reminders

How to Be a Great Player

D&D is a team game. You are not trying to beat the DM. You are telling a story with your friends.

  • Share the spotlight. Let a friend sneak, cast, or cause chaos. Then you drop a joke or a blast.
  • Listen to the DM. Details in a room description are often clues.
  • Accept failure. A 1 can become the funniest moment of the night. Even undead patrons like a good flop.
  • Be creative. "I Eldritch Blast the chandelier rope" is more fun than "I attack."
