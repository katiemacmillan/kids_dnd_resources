Chapter 1: How to Play

Wren, you are a hero in Bounties Abound. The Dungeon Master (DM) describes the world. You say what Wren does. Then the dice help decide what happens. You are on a team — share the adventure with your friends.

The Golden Rule

If a special rule on this book or your character sheet disagrees with a basic rule, use the special rule. Your spellbook and prepared spells are special rules.

The d20 Check

When you try something risky, roll a 20-sided die (d20) and add the number the DM asks for.

  1. Roll a d20.
  2. Add your bonus (like +5 for Arcana).
  3. Tell the DM the total.

If your total is equal to or higher than the Difficulty Class (DC), you succeed. Easy jobs might be DC 10. Hard jobs might be 15 or 20.

Advantage & Disadvantage

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher number.

Disadvantage: roll two d20s and keep the lower number.

If you have both at once, they cancel. Just roll one d20.

Natural 20 and Natural 1

A 20 on the die is a critical success. Something extra cool happens. A 1 is a critical fail. Something goes wrong, but it can still be funny.

Inspiration

If you play Wren in a clever, curious, or kind way, the DM may give you Inspiration. Spend it later for Advantage. You can only hold one at a time.

Chapter 2: Your Turn

When a fight starts, everyone rolls Initiative: d20 + your Dexterity modifier. Wren, that is d20 + 2. Highest total goes first.

On Your Turn You Can:

  • Move up to 30 feet.
  • Take 1 Action (Attack, Cast a Spell, Dash, Help, Hide, Search, and more).
  • Take 1 Bonus Action if a power or spell says you can.
  • Talk a little. A diagnosis, a warning, or a plan is free.

You also get 1 Reaction between your turns (for example, an Opportunity Attack if a foe runs away from you).

Useful Actions

  • Cast a Spell: your most important action most turns.
  • Attack: usually your quarterstaff, if someone is too close.
  • Dash: extra Speed this turn (another 30 feet).
  • Disengage: move away without Opportunity Attacks.
  • Help: give a friend Advantage on their next check or attack. A smart wizard Help can win a fight.
  • Hide or Ready: duck out of sight, or wait for a trigger.

Hitting and Missing

Roll d20 + your attack bonus. If you meet or beat the target's Armor Class (AC), you hit. Then roll damage. A natural 20 is a critical hit: roll the damage dice twice, then add your bonus once.

Some spells don't attack. The enemy rolls a saving throw instead. Your Spell save DC is 13. They must beat that number.

Hit Points and Rest

You have 9 Hit Points and AC 12. Stay behind a tougher friend when claws come out. At 0 HP you fall unconscious and make Death Saving Throws (d20: 10+ succeeds). Three successes stabilize you. Three failures and Wren dies. A 20 wakes you with 1 HP. A 1 counts as two failures.

A Short Rest is about 1 hour. You can spend Hit Dice to heal (you have 1d6). You can also use Arcane Recovery once per day on a short rest: regain 1 first-level slot. A Long Rest is about 8 hours. All HP and spell slots return, and you can pick new prepared spells.

Chapter 3: Scores, Skills & Dice

Your six ability scores are who Wren is in numbers. The modifier is what you add to rolls.

STR 13 (+1) DEX 14 (+2) CON 16 (+3) INT 17 (+3) WIS 15 (+2) CHA 14 (+2)

Your Proficiency Bonus is +2. Intelligence is your star stat. You are the party scholar.

Your Best Skills

  • Arcana +5: how magic works, what that rune means.
  • History +5: old wars, famous villains, "I think I've read about this."
  • Medicine +4: treat wounds, guess a poison, plague-doctor knowledge.
  • Insight +3: tell if someone is lying or scared.

Chapter 4: You Are Human

Humans are adaptable. You don't have claws or wings. You have a brain, a plan, and a mask that makes people listen. Your walking Speed is 30 feet. You speak Common and Elvish. Your beaked mask, dark coat, and careful hands are part of Wren — sniff a bottle, take notes, and stay calm when others panic.

You have no special damage resistances or vulnerabilities. You are living and breathing, so healing magic works on you normally. It usually does not work on undead (skeletons, zombies, and similar).

Versatile

Humans pick up skills quickly. You get one extra skill proficiency. Wren already used this on the character sheet (your four trained skills: Arcana, History, Medicine, and Insight).

Resourceful

After you finish a Long Rest, you get Heroic Inspiration. Spend it to reroll a d20 when you really need to save the day. You must use the new roll. If the DM also hands out Inspiration, ask whether you can hold both or just one.

Chapter 5: You Are a Wizard

Wizards learn magic by studying. Your power lives in a spellbook. Each morning you prepare the spells you think you'll need. You are a problem-solver with pockets full of answers.

Your magic uses Intelligence. Spell save DC is 13. Spell attack bonus is +5.

What You Are Great At

  • Healing and protecting (Cure Wounds, Sanctuary, Spare the Dying).
  • Controlling a fight (Entangle).
  • Hurting one big threat (Inflict Wounds).
  • Knowing things (Arcana, History, Comprehend Languages, Detect Poison and Disease).
  • Fixing gear with Mending and moving tiny objects with Mage Hand.

Spell Slots (Level 1)

You have 2 first-level spell slots. Casting a 1st-level spell spends a slot. You get them back after a Long Rest. Once per day on a Short Rest, Arcane Recovery gives you 1 slot back.

Cantrips never spend slots. Cast them as often as you want.

Preparing Spells

Your book has 6 spells. Each day after a Long Rest, pick 4 of them to have ready. You can change the list tomorrow. Cantrips are always ready. They don't count against the 4.

Growing Stronger

You are level 1. As you level up (usually through level 5), wizards typically:

  • Add two new spells to the book each level.
  • Prepare more spells.
  • Get more slots, then 2nd-level and 3rd-level magic.
  • Choose a Wizard subclass around level 3 (a specialty school or tradition).

The DM will tell you the exact new toys. Keep copying interesting spells you find on adventures if the DM says you can.

Chapter 6: Your Spellbook

Cantrips (always ready)

Spare the Dying. Action. Touch a living creature at 0 HP. They become stable (no more death saves). This does not work on undead or constructs.

Mending. 1 minute. Repair a small break or tear in an object (a split strap, a torn page, a broken mug). You cannot restore magic to a broken magic item.

Mage Hand. Action. A ghostly hand appears within 30 feet. It can carry up to 10 pounds, open an unlocked door, or scoop an item. It can't attack or carry heavy things. It lasts 1 minute.

Your Six Spells (prepare 4 each day)

Cure Wounds. Action, touch. 1d8 + 3 hit points. Your main heal. Living friends only — not undead.

Inflict Wounds. Action, touch. Spell attack +5. Hit: 3d10 necrotic. Your scariest close-range spell. You have to get next to the target.

Sanctuary. Bonus Action. A creature you touch is warded. Until the start of your next turn, anyone who tries to attack them or target them with a harmful spell must make a Wisdom save (DC 13) or pick a new target. The ward ends if the protected creature makes an attack or casts a harmful spell.

Entangle. Action. A 20-foot square of grasping weeds. Creatures in the area make a Strength save (DC 13) or are Restrained (speed 0, attacks at Disadvantage, attacks against them have Advantage). They can use an Action to try to break free (Strength check DC 13). Lasts 1 minute, concentration.

Comprehend Languages. Action. For 1 hour you understand any spoken language you hear, and you can read any written language (1 minute per page). You don't magically speak those languages back.

Detect Poison and Disease. Action. Concentration, up to 10 minutes. Sense poison, poisonous creatures, and diseases within 30 feet. You know the kind. Very plague-doctor. Walls of earth and stone can block it.

Weapon

Quarterstaff. Attack +3. Hit: 1d8 + 1 bludgeoning (two hands). Fine in a pinch. Spells are usually better.

A Simple Day Plan

If you don't know what to prepare: Cure Wounds, Entangle, Sanctuary, Inflict Wounds. Swap in Detect Poison and Disease before a swamp or sickroom. Swap in Comprehend Languages before ruins, letters, or strange voices.

In a fight: Entangle the pack, Sanctuary on a hurt friend, Cure Wounds after, Inflict Wounds only if you must step in. Cantrips keep you useful when slots are gone. You have no damaging cantrip, so Help, Ready, or your staff may be your backup.

Chapter 7: Quick Reminders

How to Be a Great Player

D&D is a team game. You are not trying to beat the DM. You are telling a story with your friends.

  • Share the spotlight. Ask what someone else notices. Let a friend try a trick. Then you drop the smart plan.
  • Listen to the DM. Room details are often clues for Arcana, History, or Medicine.
  • Accept failure. A miscast spell can still be a great story.
  • Be creative. Mage Hand can pull a lever you don't want to touch.
  • Protect the party. You are the medic. Watch everyone's HP. Undead friends need rest (or special magic), not Cure Wounds.
