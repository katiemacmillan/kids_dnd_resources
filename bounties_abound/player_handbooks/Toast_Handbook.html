Chapter 1: How to Play

Toast, you are a hero in Bounties Abound. The Dungeon Master (DM) describes the world. You say what Toast does. Then the dice help decide what happens. You are on a team — share the adventure with your friends.

The Golden Rule

If a special rule on this book or your character sheet disagrees with a basic rule, use the special rule. Your Sneak Attack and cat speed are special rules.

The d20 Check

When you try something risky, roll a 20-sided die (d20) and add the number the DM asks for.

  1. Roll a d20.
  2. Add your bonus (like +7 for Stealth).
  3. Tell the DM the total.

If your total is equal to or higher than the Difficulty Class (DC), you succeed. Easy jobs might be DC 10. Hard jobs might be 15 or 20.

Advantage & Disadvantage

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher number.

Disadvantage: roll two d20s and keep the lower number.

If you have both at once, they cancel. Just roll one d20.

Natural 20 and Natural 1

A 20 on the die is a critical success. Something extra cool happens. A 1 is a critical fail. Something goes wrong, but it can still be funny. Even a cat in a tuxedo can trip on the stairs.

Inspiration

If you play Toast in a stylish, curious, or daring way, the DM may give you Inspiration. Spend it later for Advantage. You can only hold one at a time.

Chapter 2: Your Turn

When a fight starts, everyone rolls Initiative: d20 + your Dexterity modifier. Toast, that is d20 + 5. You will often go first. Use that to set up Sneak Attack or dash into a clever spot.

On Your Turn You Can:

  • Move up to 40 feet (you are very fast).
  • Take 1 Action (Attack, Dash, Disengage, Hide, Help, Search, and more).
  • Take 1 Bonus Action if a power says you can. (Feline Agility is not a Bonus Action. It happens when you move.)
  • Talk a little. A taunt, a compliment, or "I meant to do that" is free.

You also get 1 Reaction between your turns. You might make an Opportunity Attack if a foe runs away from you.

Useful Actions

  • Attack: short sword, dagger, or claws. This is how Sneak Attack happens.
  • Dash: extra Speed this turn. Combined with Feline Agility you can cross a whole room.
  • Disengage: move away without Opportunity Attacks.
  • Hide: Stealth +7. If you are hidden, your next attack often has Advantage, which helps Sneak Attack.
  • Help: give a friend Advantage. A fancy distraction in a tuxedo counts.

Hitting and Missing

Roll d20 + your attack bonus. If you meet or beat the target's Armor Class (AC), you hit. Then roll damage. A natural 20 is a critical hit: roll the damage dice twice (including Sneak Attack dice), then add your bonus once.

Hit Points and Rest

You have 8 Hit Points and AC 15. You are hard to hit, but you are not a tank. Don't stand in a crowd of enemies. At 0 HP you fall unconscious and make Death Saving Throws (d20: 10+ succeeds). Three successes stabilize you. Three failures and Toast dies. A 20 wakes you with 1 HP. A 1 counts as two failures.

A Short Rest is about 1 hour. You can spend Hit Dice to heal (you have 1d8). Feline Agility also comes back after a short or long rest. A Long Rest is about 8 hours. You get all HP back.

Chapter 3: Scores, Skills & Dice

Your six ability scores are who Toast is in numbers. The modifier is what you add to rolls.

STR 17 (+3) DEX 20 (+5) CON 11 (+0) INT 16 (+3) WIS 14 (+2) CHA 12 (+1)

Your Proficiency Bonus is +2. Dexterity 20 is amazing. You are the fastest, sneakiest, most precise striker in the group.

Your Best Skills

  • Stealth +7: vanish in shadows, even in a nice suit.
  • Sleight of Hand +7: pick pockets, palming keys, card tricks.
  • Athletics +5: climb, jump, grapple. Cats + muscles.
  • Intimidation +5: a polite smile that is somehow scary.
  • Deception +4 and Performance +4: lies, accents, and showmanship.

Chapter 4: You Are Tabaxi

Tabaxi are cat people: curious, graceful, and always a little bit extra. You walk on two legs, wear a fancy suit, and still have claws. Your walking Speed is 40 feet. You have Darkvision out to 60 feet. You speak Common and Elvish. Cat's Talent means you are trained in Stealth (already +7 on your sheet) and Perception (Wisdom — spotting traps, hidden doors, and tasty snacks).

You have no special damage resistances or vulnerabilities. Healing magic works on you normally.

Feline Agility

When you move on your turn, you can double your Speed until the end of that turn (that's 80 feet!). You can do this once per rest (Short or Long). Think of it as a big pounce. Sprint, then catch your breath later.

Cat's Claws

Scratch attack: Strength +5, hit 1d6 + 3 slashing. You are also a great climber — ask to scale walls, wagons, and fancy furniture. Your short sword is usually better in a fight, but claws never get confiscated at the door.

Playing a Fancy Cat

You can be polite, dramatic, and a little vain. Straighten your cuffs. Be curious about shiny things. Then ruin a villain's day with a perfect sneak attack.

Chapter 5: You Are a Rogue

Rogues win with timing, not with giant swords. You look for a fair fight... and then make it unfair. You are a scout, a striker, and the person who opens the locked box.

What You Are Great At

  • Going first (Initiative +5).
  • Hitting hard once with Sneak Attack.
  • Sneaking, climbing, and stealing.
  • Looking fabulous while doing it.

Sneak Attack (1d6)

Once per turn, when you hit with a finesse or ranged weapon, you can add 1d6 extra damage if either:

  • You have Advantage on the attack, or
  • Another enemy of the target is within 5 feet of it (a friend is in its face), you don't have Disadvantage, and you don't have to be hidden.

Your short sword and dagger are finesse weapons. Claws are not finesse, so Sneak Attack does not apply to claw attacks. Use the sword when you want the extra dice.

On a critical hit, you roll the Sneak Attack die twice as well.

Coming at Level 2: Cunning Action

Right now you are level 1, so you spend your Action to Dash, Disengage, or Hide. At level 2, rogues usually get Cunning Action: do one of those as a Bonus Action instead. That will make you even slipperier. Until then, pick your Action carefully.

Growing Stronger

As you level up (usually through level 5), rogues typically:

  • Level 2: Cunning Action.
  • Level 3: a Rogue subclass (a special style) and more Sneak Attack (2d6).
  • Level 4: a little stronger and quicker.
  • Level 5: Uncanny Dodge (halve one hit with your Reaction) and Sneak Attack 3d6.

The DM will tell you the exact features. For now, be the tuxedo blur.

Chapter 6: Your Moves

Weapons

Short Sword. Attack +7. Hit: 1d6 + 3 slashing. Finesse. This is your Sneak Attack weapon.

Dagger. Attack +7. Hit: 1d4 + 3 piercing. Finesse. You can throw it (range 20/60 feet) if you need a ranged sneak.

Cat's Claws. Attack +5. Hit: 1d6 + 3 slashing. No Sneak Attack. Good if you are disarmed or climbing and swiping.

A Simple Combat Plan

  1. Stand near a friend who is next to the same monster, OR Hide first for Advantage.
  2. Attack with the short sword. Add 1d6 Sneak Attack if you qualify.
  3. If you need to get there this turn, use Feline Agility to double your Speed.
  4. Don't eat hits on purpose. AC 15 is good. 8 HP is not.

Out of combat, you are the lockpick and the scout. Ask to sneak ahead. Check for traps. Charm people in the fancy suit. If a shiny object is unattended... well, you are a cat.

Chapter 7: Quick Reminders

How to Be a Great Player

D&D is a team game. You are not trying to beat the DM. You are telling a story with your friends.

  • Share the spotlight. If someone else is also sneaky, take turns being the star thief.
  • Listen to the DM. Shadows, chandeliers, and tablecloths are playground equipment.
  • Accept failure. A missed jump can still look cinematic.
  • Be creative. "I knock the candelabra onto the rug and hide in the smoke" is very Toast.
  • Protect squishy friends. Get next to a friend's target so you both can do your jobs.
