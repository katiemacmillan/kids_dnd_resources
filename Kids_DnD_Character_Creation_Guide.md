# Make Your Own Hero!
## A Step-by-Step Character Creation Guide for Kids

**For players ages 9–12 · Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition**

You just finished an adventure with pre-made heroes. Now it is **your turn** to invent someone totally new — your name, your look, your personality, your powers. Work through this booklet **one step at a time**, then give your sheet to the DM so they can check the numbers.

**You will also get:** the **Basic Rules** booklet, a **Species Handbook**, a **Class Handbook**, and (if your class uses magic or gear lists) a **Spell Book** or **Gear Book**.

---

## Before You Start: Three Big Ideas

1. **Your character is a team player.** You are brave, funny, or clever — but you care about your friends.
2. **Failure is fun.** If you roll low, something silly or exciting usually happens. That is part of the game!
3. **There are no wrong ideas.** A pastry knight, a nervous filing clerk, and a bronze golem can all be heroes. Pick what makes *you* excited.

## Step 4 — Assign Your Ability Scores

Every hero has **six ability scores**. They measure how good you are at different things.

| Ability | What it means | Good for… |
|---------|---------------|-----------|
| **Strength (STR)** | 💪 Muscle | Big weapons, breaking doors, climbing |
| **Dexterity (DEX)** | 🏃 Speed & reflexes | Bows, sneaking, dodging |
| **Constitution (CON)** | 🛡️ Toughness | Hit points, surviving poison |
| **Intelligence (INT)** | 🧠 Book smarts | Wizards, investigation, history |
| **Wisdom (WIS)** | 🦉 Instinct & awareness | Clerics, druids, noticing traps |
| **Charisma (CHA)** | ✨ Charm & leadership | Bards, paladins, talking to NPCs |

### How to get your six numbers

Your DM will give you one of these methods:

**Method A — The Fair Six (Standard Array)**  
You get these six numbers: **15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8**  
Assign each number to one ability. Put the **big numbers** on abilities your class uses most!

**Method B — Roll for Drama**  
Roll **4d6** (four six-sided dice), drop the lowest die, add the rest. Do this **six times**. Ask the DM to help if any number seems too low — they may let you reroll ones.

**Method C — The DM Helps**  
Describe your hero and the DM suggests scores that fit.

### Where to put your numbers (cheat sheet)

| Class | Put your best scores in… |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Fighter, Barbarian, Paladin | STR, CON |
| Rogue, Ranger, Monk | DEX, then WIS or STR |
| Wizard | INT, CON |
| Sorcerer, Warlock, Bard | CHA, CON |
| Cleric, Druid | WIS, CON |

**Species bonus:** Some species add +2 or +1 to certain abilities. Add those *after* you assign numbers. Check your Species Handbook!

## Step 8 — AC, HP, Speed, and Attacks

### Armor Class (AC)

**AC** = how hard you are to hit. Enemies must roll **your AC or higher** on a d20 to hurt you.

### Hit Points (HP)

**HP** = your health. At **0 HP**, you fall unconscious (but your friends can still save you!).

**At level 1:**  
HP = **hit die maximum** + CON modifier  
(Example: Fighter d10 → 10 + CON mod)

**Each level after:**  
Add **hit die roll** (or average) + CON modifier each time you level up. Your DM helps at the table.

**My HP:** current / max

**My hit die:** d_____ (e.g. d8 for a rogue)

**My speed:** _____ ft. (usually 30; some species are faster or slower)

### Attacks

**To hit:** d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus (if proficient with that weapon)

**Damage:** weapon damage die + ability modifier (usually STR for melee, DEX for finesse/ranged)

## Step 9 — Spells (Magic Users Only)

Skip this step if your class does **not** cast spells (Fighter, Barbarian, Rogue without magic, etc.).

| Kind | What it means |
|------|----------------|

## Step 10 — Personality, Background, and Quirk

This is the **most important step** for making your hero feel real.

### Background

Pick or invent a **background** — what your hero did before adventuring.

| Background idea | You were… |
|-----------------|-----------|
| **Folk Hero** | A village favorite who did something brave |
| **Scholar** | A student, librarian, or inventor |
| **Soldier** | A guard, knight-in-training, or soldier |
| **Urchin** | Grew up on the streets, quick and scrappy |
| **Acolyte** | Served at a temple or shrine |
| **Entertainer** | Actor, musician, or circus performer |
| **Judge** | Sat on a bench, heard both sides, and decided the truth |
| **Custom** | Invent your own! (Ask the DM) |

**My background:**

**One skill or tool it gives me:**

#### If you pick Judge

You used to decide cases. You were probably the quiet kind: people argued, you listened, then you said one short sentence and that was the verdict.

- **Skills:** Insight and Investigation (if you don't already have them).
- **Feature — The Last Word:** In a town, castle, or camp, if people are arguing about a crime, a rule, or who is lying, they often let you speak last — and they listen. You can also get a short meeting with a mayor, a captain of the guard, or a local judge by explaining you used to sit the bench. They don't have to obey you. They do have to hear you.
- **How to play it:** Talk in short sentences. Give people one chance. You hate bullies more than thieves.
